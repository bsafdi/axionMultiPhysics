    Particle transport validation (beta)¶

    Particle transport (axionmultiphysics.particles and axmp-transport, see the guide) is beta. This page lists every check of the transport against an independent reference, what its last run measured, with its seed and number of events, and what is not checked. The checks are the tests tests/test_particles_transport.py, tests/test_particles_demo.py and tests/test_particles_driver.py; the references are written from physics and the design note in tests/_particles_ref.py, never from the implementation.

    Run: Geant4 11.4.3 (multithreaded, tasking run manager), FTFP_BERT, production cut 0.7 mm, Geant4's default integrator (G4TDormandPrince45 with G4IntegrationDriver, deltaChord 0.25 mm, deltaOneStep 0.01 mm, deltaIntersection 1 um, epsilon 5e-5 to 1e-3), the sequential build of the package, AMD nodes of the cluster's cm1 and cm2 partitions (identical values on both), AXMP_SLOW=1. A single track is deterministic; every other number is Monte Carlo, given with its statistical error (one standard deviation: of the binomial count; of a standard deviation from N samples, 1/sqrt(2(N - 1)) relative; of a Gaussian core of N events, by 200 bootstrap resamplings of the events, 0.9 % relative at N = 1e4, about 1.35 / sqrt(2N), which is the value given for P5). Tolerances are the design note's ceilings or the tests' own; the measured value is given beside them.

    The driver's own guarantees (seeds, the order of the merge, extension, prefixes and partial runs, retries, the 128-bit sums, the estimators and their printing) are checked bit for bit with a stand-in program in tests/test_particles_driver.py, including S3 at the level of the estimator (1000 experiments drawn by numpy) and S4 (0 of 10 and of 1e4).

    The 3D iron dipole of the demonstration skips: its test calls particles.transport without solids=, which this version does not serve (capabilities()["partition_export"] is false), although the mesher exports 3D partitions (Design.export_gdml).

    Test status¶

    Every assertion of the three files passes on the program and the driver of this version (the 3D dipole and, where the application is absent, X1 skip).

    X1 (the solids list read back from the exported GDML file by a standalone Geant4 application, axmp-x1, with a Geant4 built with GDML and Xerces-C 3.3.0) applies the same exit and hit rules as the program (interfaces are not exits; a step after a zero-length step records no hit). Against the transport as it was during the development of this version, with the interface rule in place, its three tests passed: volumes and materials bitwise equal, 1e4 geantino crossings and their first random numbers bitwise equal, per-region mean deposits of 2000 electrons of 200 MeV in 0.3 T within 4 sigma (and per-event deposits bitwise equal). The comparison has not been run again against the program of this release, which added the handling of stuck tracks, the per-writer temporary directories and the checks of the gun afterwards.

    What is not validated¶

    • No comparison with measured data, and no estimate of the physics-list dependence (the three lists run; their differences are not quantified).
    • The convergence evidence of the design note's rule 3 (results with the production cut, the step and the map tolerance halved) is missing; it is left to the next milestone.
    • X1 runs only where its application and a Geant4 built with GDML exist; its tests skip elsewhere.
    • The energy balance of an event (deposits plus escaping energy against the primary's) is not checked: decays and hadronic interactions change the rest-mass energy, which the per-event arrays do not account for.
    • The 3D dipole of the demonstration, and the transport through 3D (tessellated) solids lists in general (they are built and checked by --geometry only).
    • Field maps of 3D solves through the transport (kind-3 maps are checked with analytic fields only).
