    Field maps for particle transport (beta)¶

    A particle tracker needs the static magnetic field at arbitrary points, many millions of times, without the finite element solve. Results.export_field_map writes the field of a solved design as a field map: one regular grid (a patch) per region, each holding that region's own recovered field, refined until a fixed set of line integrals through the region agrees with the field to a stated tolerance. axionmultiphysics.fieldmap reads, evaluates and checks such maps. The format is axionmultiphysics.fieldmap/1.

    Field maps are beta: their interface may change. The error control was calibrated on solved axisymmetric fields (a permeable sphere, superconducting and thin-layer sheets, iron cores, solenoids, a knee-law toroid) and checked on one 3D solve; the transport that reads the maps is not part of this version.

    Exporting a map¶

    import axionmultiphysics as axmp

res = axmp.Design(doc).solve(workdir="runs/magnet")
fm = res.export_field_map("runs/magnet/fieldmap", tol=1e-4,
                          track_volumes=[{"name": "bore", "r": [0.0, 0.2], "z": [-0.5, 0.5]}])
print([(p.name, p.shape, p.record["accepted"]) for p in fm.patches])   # a patch not accepted also warns
B = fm.evaluate([[0.05, 0.0, 0.1]], patch="air+winding")   # Cartesian (x, y, z) in m -> (Bx, By, Bz) in T
    option default meaning
    regions None the regions to map; None maps every region except ideal superconductors
    tol 1e-4 the RMS error of the map's chord integrals each patch must reach (0 < tol < 1)
    merge True adjacent regions with the same constitutive law and no curve between them share one patch
    track_volumes None boxes whose own chord families are reported: {"name", "r": [lo, hi], "z": [lo, hi]} (3D: "x", "y", "z")
    iterate_on_track_volumes False refine until the track volumes, too, meet the tolerance
    outside "refuse" what the evaluator does outside a patch's box: "refuse" or "zero"
    max_patch_MB, max_total_MB 512.0, 2048.0 caps on one patch's array and on all of them (MB = 2^20 bytes)

    Axisymmetric results give kind 1 patches: (B_r, B_z, B_φ) on an (r, z) grid, interpolated bilinearly; Results3D gives kind 3 patches: (B_x, B_y, B_z) on a Cartesian grid, interpolated trilinearly. Planar and cross-section results are not mapped. A refusal writes nothing: neither path nor path.tmp exists afterwards.

    What a patch holds¶

    One patch per region. Each region is served by its own grid, which covers the region's bounding box. Adjacent regions whose constitutive laws are equal (linear with μ_r equal within 1e-6 relative, such as air, windings and normal conductors, or the same nonlinear material) and whose shared faces carry no curve merge into one patch, named by its regions joined by + ("air+winding"). Ideal superconducting regions get no patch (B = 0 there) and are listed under zero_field_regions.

    Curves. A superconducting sheet or a thin magnetic layer inside a region is an interface: the field jumps across it. A closed one splits the region into parts, each a patch of its own named <name>#k. An open one, which has the same part of the region on both of its sides, is refused with fieldmap.curve_in_region: no patch can hold a jump that ends inside it. Close the curve, or leave the region out. A sheet of a normal conductor (copper, say) is transparent to the static field: it is not an interface and is ignored (no split, no refusal, not listed in curves).

    The recovered field B*. Within a patch the map represents B*, the finite element field B_h averaged at the nodes of a continuous space of the solution's order over the patch's own elements only, so averaging never crosses an interface with a jump. On the axis (kind 1, r = 0) B* has B_r = B_φ = 0, and B_z is the mean of the limits B_z = 2 ∂A_φ/∂r of the elements that have an edge on the axis.

    Nodes. A grid node inside or on a patch element takes B* there (one-sided at the patch boundary). A node outside the patch whose grid cells reach the bounding box of a patch element takes the polynomial of the nearest patch element, continued to the node (never a neighbour's value), so that the stepper's trial points just outside the region see the region's own field continued. Other nodes hold 0. Kind-1 axis nodes hold B_r = B_φ = 0. The patch's box is the box of its elements' nodes, so a region whose faces lie on the box (a rectangular core) has no grid beyond them: points just outside are served by clamping (below).

    How far the continuation can be trusted. Where a region's boundary lies inside the box (a curved interface, a sheet), the continued polynomial reaches about one cell of the final level past the boundary, and beyond that the grid holds zeros, which are interpolated without any refusal. In the sphere example (tolerance 1e-3) the air patch evaluated inside the ball, against the exact exterior field continued, differs from it by at most 0.8 % of B_patch at 0.25 final cell past the boundary and by up to 27 % at 1 cell (median 1.1 %); at 2 cells the error is large (median 14 %) and at 4 cells most points return 0. The depth shrinks as the map is refined, and the margin (below) does not extend it: the margin applies only where a region face is the box face. A stepper that visits points beyond a curved interface must keep them within a fraction of a final-level cell.

    Field-free patches. A patch whose B_patch (below) is at most 1e-10 of the largest B_patch of the map, such as the inside of a closed superconducting sheet, is written as zeros (field_free: true) and not iterated on.

    Error control¶

    Grids. Level 0 of a patch has n = max(9, ceil(L / h_el) + 1) nodes along each axis of its box (length L), with h_el the median element size of the patch (sqrt(2 × area) in 2D, (6 × volume)^(1/3) in 3D); each level halves the spacing (n → 2n − 1).

    Chords. Each patch has fixed families of straight chords of nominal length 8 h_el, clipped to the patch (and, for kind 1, to r ≥ 0): bulk (16384, start points spread over all elements), layer (4096, start points within h_el of the patch's jump boundary: faces whose other side is another region or another part across a curve; not the outer boundary of the domain, not the axis), axis (4096, kind-1 patches that touch r = 0, start points with r < h_el) and track_<name> (4096 per track volume that meets the patch, clipped to the box). Kind-1 chords are lines in the (r, z) half-plane; kind-3 chords have isotropic directions. Start points are drawn element by element (probability ∝ area or volume) from a scrambled-Halton sequence whose seed is a constant of the format (chord_seed); the same chords serve every level.

    The statistic. With B_patch the largest |B*| over the B* nodal values of the patch's elements, per chord c

    e_c = |∫_c (B_map − B*) dl| / max( ∫_c |B*| dl , 0.1 L_c B_patch ),
n_c = |∫_c (B*  − B_h) dl| / the same denominator,

    and, per family, E = RMS over chords of e_c (also its 95 % quantile and maximum) and H = RMS of n_c. The integrals of the map are exact (multilinear along a line); those of B*, |B*| and B_h use adaptive Gauss-Kronrod quadrature on the pieces between element boundaries. A chord is its intersection with the patch, found by walking it element by element (curved elements included): nothing of it is ever left out. An excursion shorter than the sampling step of the walk's point location (about 1e-5 m of closest approach to a curved interface) is not resolved: a chord that barely grazes the interface is counted as staying in the patch, which in the measured cases added up to a few mm (0.5-1 % of the chord) of the neighbouring region. A walk that cannot be completed (no element past a face, or more than a few tens of consecutive hand-overs without advancing along the chord) stops the export with fieldmap.chord_incomplete and writes nothing, rather than shortening the chord. Before any chord is drawn, a patch with an element whose Jacobian determinant is not of one sign at the nodes or the points between them (a tangled element) is refused with fieldmap.tangled_mesh. This is a second guard: the mesher repairs or refuses a 2D mesh with a tangled element, and the solve refuses one it reads (mesh.tangled, see "Curved elements in 2D" in the design format), so a solved design does not reach it.

    Acceptance. A patch is accepted when E ≤ tol_eff = max(tol, 0.1 H) in every acceptance family (bulk, layer, axis; the track families too with iterate_on_track_volumes). The floor 0.1 H keeps the map from resolving the field ten times better than the solve represents it: when it acts, the family's record has floor_active: true and a note with both numbers ("the map tolerance was raised to a tenth of the FE indicator ...; a finer or higher-order solve lowers H"). H is an indicator of the solve's inter-element inconsistency, not evidence of its accuracy.

    Guard and caps. From level 2 on, a family still above its tol_eff whose E fell by less than a factor 3.0 over the last two halvings stops the patch with the warning fieldmap.not_converging (written, not accepted). A patch whose next level would exceed max_patch_MB is written at the finest level that fits with fieldmap.cap_reached; a patch whose coarsest level does not fit is refused (fieldmap.patch_too_large), and so are patches that together exceed max_total_MB (fieldmap.total_cap_exceeded). A patch that is written but not accepted also raises a fieldmap.FieldMapWarning (a UserWarning) whose message starts with the code and names the patch, so that a script that does not read accepted still hears of it.

    What the accuracy is for. A momentum scale inherits δ(∫B dl)/∫B dl. E is an RMS over a patch: along tracks confined to a small part of it the map error can be ten to twenty times larger, which is what track volumes measure. That the finite element field itself is accurate enough is a question for the solve (a finer mesh or a higher order), not for the map.

    Outside the maps¶

    FieldMap.evaluate(points, patch=name) interpolates the named patch's grid (a map with one patch needs no name). The evaluator never decides which region a point is in: the transport does, by the volume it is tracking in. Each patch record carries a margin (its level-0 spacing per axis; for write_map, the written spacing): a point outside the patch's box by at most the margin along every axis is clamped onto the box and gets exactly the value of the box's nearest point (a stepper's trial point just outside a volume whose faces lie on the box). A point farther out raises fieldmap.outside_map when the map was written with outside="refuse"; with outside="zero" it gets B = 0 and is counted in FieldMap.outside_calls. An export with outside="zero" records boundary_ratio, the largest |B*| on the outer boundary of the mapped domain relative to the largest B_patch, and warns with fieldmap.outside_field_not_small above 1e-3.

    The directory format¶

    path/
  manifest.json
  patch_0/B.npy                       # shape (n_r, n_z, 3) or (n_x, n_y, n_z, 3), little-endian float64, C order, T
  patch_0/chords/<family>/start.npy   # and direction, segments, segment_chord, length, I_star, I_abs, I_h, I_map
  patch_1/...

    Patches appear in the manifest sorted by name, in directories patch_<k> in that order; node i of axis a sits at origin[a] + i * spacing[a] (m). The manifest records the schema, units (m, T), tol, outside, the chord constants (chord_seed, chords), the control constants (control), the source (fe_solution: symmetry, design name and SHA-256 of the design document, SHA-256 of the static solution, order, elements, DOFs, version and build_info without the thread count, which timing records, and without the build's directory and file names), zero_field_regions, track_volumes, warnings, the patches and timing. Each patch record has its file and SHA-256, kind and frame (kind 3: a 4 × 4 placement transform, global = T local), regions, part (component) and bounding curves, box, h_el, grid (origin, spacing, shape), the history of E per level, B_patch, field_free, accepted, stop_reason (accepted, not_converging, cap or field_free), one record per chord family (n, E, E_q95, E_max, H, tol_eff, floor_active, gain_last, gain_two, acceptance), a pointwise report at 1e5 points (|B_map − B*| and |B* − B_h| relative to B_patch: 50 %, 99 %, maximum) and the normal-component mismatch with each neighbouring patch at the midpoints of their shared faces. The same results exported twice, with any number of threads, give identical arrays and manifests apart from timing; no string of a manifest outside timing is a local path.

    fieldmap.write_map(path, patches, outside=...) writes the same format from nodal values given by the caller (an analytic field), with source: {"type": "nodal_values"}; fieldmap.load(path) reads either.

    Checking a map against its solve¶

    • fieldmap.recovered_field(results, map, patch, points): B* of a patch at points in its native coordinates ((r, z) or (x, y, z)), NaN outside its elements.
    • fieldmap.chord_error(results, map, seed=None, patch=None): E and H of every family recomputed from the map read back from disk; with the manifest's seed it reproduces the report, with another seed it gives an independent estimate. Agreement within a factor 2.5 was measured on smooth cases (a sphere, sheets, a solenoid, a knee-law toroid); next to the singular corners of a saturated iron core E differed by up to 3.24 (the core) and 7.0 (the air beside it) between seeds, because a few millimetre chords at the corners hold most of E² there.
    • fieldmap.decide(E, tol_eff) is the refinement rule itself; fieldmap.halton(seed, n) and fieldmap.family_seed(seed, index) give the chord sequences.

    Error codes¶

    code when
    fieldmap.invalid_option a bad argument (tolerance, regions, caps, track volume, outside, a patch of write_map), an unknown or superconducting region in regions, a multi-patch evaluate without patch, planar or cross-section results
    fieldmap.curve_in_region a curve with the same part of its region on both sides (an open sheet)
    fieldmap.track_volume_empty a track volume that meets no mapped patch
    fieldmap.patch_too_large a patch whose coarsest grid exceeds max_patch_MB
    fieldmap.total_cap_exceeded the written patches together exceed max_total_MB
    fieldmap.outside_map evaluate at a point outside the patch's box by more than its margin, with outside="refuse"
    fieldmap.tangled_mesh a patch with tangled curved elements (Jacobian determinant changing sign, vanishing or inverted; a curved region much thinner than the mesh size at geometry order 2); refine the mesh or use geometry order 1; nothing is written. A second guard: a solve refuses such a mesh first (mesh.tangled)
    fieldmap.chord_incomplete a chord could not be walked through the mesh (an error of the builder: report it); nothing is written
    fieldmap.not_converging warning in the manifest: the two-halving guard stopped a patch
    fieldmap.cap_reached warning in the manifest: a patch was written at the largest level that fits its cap
    fieldmap.outside_field_not_small warning in the manifest: with outside="zero", the field on the mapped domain's boundary exceeds 1e-3 of the largest B_patch

    Each error is an AxionMultiPhysicsError with code, a message naming the region, curve, patch or option, and a hint.

    Limitations¶

    • Bilinear and trilinear maps are not divergence-free; that matters only for tracking over very many turns.
    • The patch statistic is an RMS: a localized error is diluted by the patch's volume (track volumes report it).
    • The indicator H under-reports the finite element error (by up to about 14 in the measured cases).
    • Thin curved 3D shells waste most of their box and may reach the cap.
    • Next to singular corners (re-entrant iron, saturated cores) E is carried by a few chords and varies between chord seeds by factors of 3 to 7; it is reported, not gated.
    • Not in this version (described in the design): excluding the element around a re-entrant corner from the pointwise statistics, and copying the design's own convergence evidence (order 2 against order 3) into the manifest. The pointwise report includes the corner elements.
    • A placement (transform) is a rotation and a translation; write_map refuses a scale, shear or reflection.
