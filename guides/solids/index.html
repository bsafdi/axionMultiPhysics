    Solids for particle transport (beta)¶

    Geant4 transports particles through a tree of solids, each placed in its mother. axionmultiphysics.solids builds that tree from the mesher's description of an axisymmetric partition (the regions as disjoint faces in the (r, z) half-plane, bounded by curves whose exact parameters the mesher states) or of a 3D partition (the volumes of a 3D design, bounded by triangulated faces). It writes the tree as solids.json (schema axionmultiphysics.solids/1), which axmp-transport instantiates, or as GDML for any Geant4 application. Per region it reports the volume and the mass of the exact solid of revolution and of the solids that represent it. Nothing here needs gmsh or Geant4.

    Beta

    The module, the format of solids.json and the codes may change between minor versions. The partition comes from a hand-built table, from the mesher's info file of a bounded axisymmetric mode, or from an axisymmetric or 3D electromagnetic design (Design.export_gdml, 3D designs); the route from a model document to the partition comes later.

    from axionmultiphysics import solids

doc = solids.from_partition(partition, materials, region_materials)   # chord_tol_m=None
solids.write(doc, "solids.json")
solids.write_gdml(doc, "design.gdml")
    • partition: the mesher's info file of a bounded axisymmetric mode, or the file of axmp-mesher --export-regions (a JSON object), with the extended entity table described below; for a 3D design, the file of --export-regions on its 3D mesher input (3D designs).
    • materials: the document's materials, {name: {"density": kg/m3, "particle": {...}}}. Other keys of a material (its electromagnetic or thermal properties) are ignored.
    • region_materials: {region: material name} for every region that has a face.
    • chord_tol_m: the chord tolerance of every region, in m; by default each region has its own (below). A 3D partition takes none (ValueError): its triangulation is the mesher's, made to the chord_tol_m given to the export.

    solids.default_chord_tol(size_m) is min(1 µm, 1e-5 × size); solids.VOLUME_WARN is 1e-4; solids.SCHEMA is "axionmultiphysics.solids/1". Errors are AxionMultiPhysicsError with a code and a hint; the volume warning is a solids.SolidsWarning.

    The partition¶

    The conversion reads, from the info file:

    • geometry: "axisymmetric" (a planar partition is refused with particle.planar_not_offered);
    • regions: {name: {"attr": ...}}; the regions are reported in the order of attr;
    • entities: the curves, each with tag, type (line, circle or ellipse), side ([the region on the left of the curve's direction, the region on its right], "exterior" outside the domain), and the extension below;
    • faces: [{"tag", "region", "loops": [[signed tags], ...]}], every final face of the partition with its outer loop first. A loop keeps its face on its left (the outer loop counter-clockwise in (r, z), the inner loops clockwise); a positive tag runs the entity from start to end, a negative one from end to start.

    Each entity carries start and end ([r, z, 0], in m): the coordinates of its end vertices, start first along the curve. Entities that meet share the coordinates of their common vertex bit for bit, a point on the axis has r exactly 0, and a closed curve has start == end. An arc (circle or ellipse) also has center, radii ([a, b], a ≥ b; equal for a circle, also for the circular arcs that gmsh reports as ellipses), major_axis (the unit vector of the semi-axis a) and ccw (true when the curve turns counter-clockwise from start to end): the curve is c + a cos t u + b sin t v, with v the major axis turned by +90°.

    Every other key of the info file (the regions' measures, the atoms, the boundaries, the timings) is ignored, and the output depends only on the geometry: not on the order of the entities, faces or inner loops, the tags, or where a loop starts. A table that does not describe closed faces is refused with particle.partition_invalid: no faces; a face that names an entity or a region the table does not have; an entity type other than line, circle or ellipse (an imported mesh has no exact curves); a missing start or end; a loop that does not close (consecutive curves must share their vertex bit for bit); a face that runs a curve with its region on the wrong side; an outer loop that is not counter-clockwise.

    Electromagnetic designs¶

    Design.export_gdml(path, composition, *, solids=None, chord_tol_m=None, workdir=None, mesher=None) (beta) writes the GDML of an axisymmetric electromagnetic design, and returns the solids document:

    import axionmultiphysics as axmp

design = axmp.template("core")
composition = {"winding": {"nist": "G4_Cu", "density": 8960.0}, "nb": {"nist": "G4_Nb"},
               "vacuum": {"nist": "G4_Galactic", "density": 1e-22}, "air": {"nist": "G4_AIR", "density": 1.205}}
doc = design.export_gdml("core.gdml", composition, solids="solids.json")

    The route is the design's mesher input (the one axmp mesh writes), axmp-mesher --export-regions on it, and solids.from_partition on the partition it writes. The mesher's export builds the geometry of the electromagnetic mesh (the half disk, the regions in order, the curves, the fragmentation) and writes, instead of a mesh, the partition of that geometry to the info path of its input: regions (the design's regions and the background air, each with its attr, its area_m2 and its volume_m3, computed from the exact curves), entities (every curve with the keys above; side names a region, air for the background, or exterior beyond the outer arc and the axis) and faces. The tags are those of the mesh of the same input. A contour's elliptical segment, a B-spline in gmsh, is an ellipse with the contour's own centre and axes. The mesher exits with status 1 and {"ok": false, "error": ...} in that file when the construction fails, and refuses inputs of the bounded and plane modes (use the bounded mode's own info file with resolve_only: it carries the same keys). A 3D input has its own partition (3D designs).

    • composition: {material: block}, the block of Materials with an optional density in kg/m3. Each region takes its design material; the background region takes the material named air (the design's vacuum material of that name, or the design's own definition of air). Entries for materials no region uses are ignored; sheets (curves) have no volume and need no entry; disabled regions are not in the partition.
    • The density of a material is the design's, as the electromagnetic parser resolves it (iron 7874, copper 8960 and nb 8570 are built in; an expression is evaluated), when it is positive; otherwise the density of the block. Two different positive values are particle.composition_invalid (naming the material and both values); none is particle.density_missing; a used material without a block is particle.composition_missing.
    • solids: a path; the solids document is also written there as solids.json. chord_tol_m is passed to the conversion, and so are its warnings.
    • workdir: keeps regions_in.json (the mesher input) and regions.json (the partition, the mesher's info) there. Without it a temporary directory is used and removed; nothing is written in the current directory.
    • mesher: the program (default: $AXMP_MESHER, else the package's).

    Refused, with nothing written: a model document (model.em_only_operation); a 3D design with mesh.symmetry, a design on an imported mesh (mesh.file) and a mesher without the export mode (particle.partition_unavailable); a mesher that cannot be run or loaded, as the model path reports it (build.requires_mesher, with the loader's message); a planar or cross-section design (particle.planar_not_offered); a mesher run that fails (mesh.generation_failed, the message carrying the mesher's error and the last lines of its log in the hint; with workdir the log stays there as regions_mesher.log). The input is made by the code that axmp mesh runs, which also meshes the design once into a temporary directory.

    3D designs¶

    Design.export_gdml also exports a 3D electromagnetic design ("symmetry": "3d"), with the same arguments and the same composition rules. Every volume becomes a tessellated solid: a closed triangulated surface, which Geant4 builds as a G4TessellatedSolid. The route is the design's 3D mesher input (the mesh3d_in.json that axmp mesh writes, made by a library function that the 3D engine also uses, compiled into every build: the sequential build exports 3D designs too, and the engine is not run), axmp-mesher --export-regions on it and solids.from_partition on the partition. Each rank of a parallel program (mpirun -n 4 python script.py) exports on its own, with the same files; nothing is collective. The mesher runs only with the export flag, never for an electromagnetic mesh. The engine's own refusals of a design (sheets, in a 3D design) are reported as themselves. A design reduced by symmetry planes (mesh.symmetry) is refused with particle.partition_unavailable: a reduced model is not the device.

    The mesher builds the 3D geometry as for the electromagnetic mesh (the solids, the domain sphere, the infinity shell, the fragmentation, the region of each volume by priority) and writes, instead of a mesh, the 3D partition to the info path of its input. The infinity shell is not part of it: the transport's domain ends at the domain sphere, the inner radius domain_radius. The conductors that the electromagnetic mesh removes (remove_pec) are volumes of the partition. The file has geometry "3d", domain_radius, domain_center, diagonal_m (of the union of the faces' boxes), tolerance_m (1e-9 × diagonal_m), chord_tol_m and:

    • regions: {name: {"attr", "volume_m3"}} for the design's regions and the background air; volume_m3 is the exact volume (OpenCASCADE's).
    • entities: one record per face, in the order of tag, with dim 2, side ([A, B]: the face's triangles' normal points from the region A into the region B; air for the background, exterior beyond the domain sphere, where the infinity shell was), curves ([]), axis (false), type (gmsh's surface type in lower case: plane, cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, bspline_surface, ...), lo and hi (a box that holds the face), point and normal (a point of the face and the unit normal of the surface there, turned like the triangles) and area_m2.
    • volumes: [{"tag", "region", "shells", "volume_m3"}], every volume but the shell's. tag is the volume's tag in the mesh of the same input. shells lists signed face tags, the outer shell first: +tag where the volume is the face's side[0] (the triangles' normal points out of it), -tag where it is side[1]. A shell with its faces turned by their signs is a closed manifold surface oriented outwards (every edge used once in each direction in the shell); a cavity is an inner shell. Faces belong to one shell when an edge of the shell pairs them, so the air around two boxes that meet along an edge has a shell for each. A domain without regions exports the air alone (one volume).
    • triangulation: {"nodes": [[x, y, z], ...], "faces": [{"tag", "triangles", "sagitta_m"}, ...]}. One node list serves the whole partition; each face is triangulated once and listed once (the volumes on its two sides use the same triangles, one with the reversed orientation, and the faces that meet at a curve share its nodes, so that coordinates agree bit for bit with no matching tolerance). sagitta_m is the largest distance of a triangle's centroid or edge midpoint from the face (0 for a plane).

    The chord tolerance. chord_tol_m is the largest distance a triangle may lie from its face. It is the input's key chord_tol_m (the chord_tol_m of Design.export_gdml), by default min(1 mm, 1e-4 × diagonal_m). The triangulation is the export's own, made for that tolerance, and depends on the geometry and the tolerance only, not on the sizes of the electromagnetic mesh. A region's triangulated volume differs from its exact one only under the triangles of curved faces (reported per region, and warned about above VOLUME_WARN: lower chord_tol_m); planar regions are exact to rounding.

    The solids. One solid per volume, of kind tessellated with params {"vertices_m": [[x, y, z], ...], "triangles": [[i, j, k], ...]}: the triangles of the volume's outer shell, counter-clockwise seen from outside, with its own vertex list copied bit for bit from the partition's nodes, so that solids that touch have the same facets on the face between them. Position and rotation are zero (the vertices are absolute); discretized is true and sagitta_m the largest of the outer shell's faces. Names are <region>_<k> in a geometric order. The volumes in a cavity of a volume (inside one of its inner shells, directly or as a cluster of volumes touching across faces of their outer shells) are that volume's daughters; a volume in no cavity is a daughter of the world, a vacuum orb around every node. In the report, volume_solids_m3 is the volume of the region's solids minus their daughters', which is the triangulated volume of the region.

    Thin layers. The triangles are chords of the exact surfaces, so a layer thinner than about the chord tolerance cannot be represented: the chords of its two surfaces cross. The conversion refuses such a partition with particle.partition_invalid (the message names the region, the hint says to lower chord_tol_m well below the layer's thickness) when a daughter's triangulated surface is not inside its mother's, when two siblings overlap, or when a solid's own triangles cross each other. Triangles that two solids share exactly are contact, not overlap. The mesher still exports the partition at any tolerance (its sides are facts of the topology), and the same partition converts at a finer chord_tol_m. The checks use a sweep over the triangles' boxes, exact piercing tests of triangle edges against triangles (the tolerance is tolerance_m) and ray casting for containment; on the window-frame dipole (29 000 triangles) they take about 2 s. Pairs of triangles are found with a uniform 3D grid, so the time grows about as the number of triangles; a solid that is star-shaped about the centre of its box (a ball, a box, a cylinder: every triangle faces away from the centre and their solid angles add up to 4 pi) cannot cross itself and skips the self-crossing test. A fine chord_tol_m costs mostly in triangles, not in the checks: the count of a curved face grows as 1 / chord_tol_m (a sphere of 0.5 m radius: 64 000 triangles at 1.5e-4 m, 425 000 at 3.3e-5 m, converted in 1.7 s and 8.9 s with the self-crossing test forced on, 0.2 s and 1.1 s without), and the export, the JSON files and Geant4's tessellated solids grow with it.

    GDML. A tessellated solid is a <tessellated name="<gdml_name>_solid" lunit="m"> of <triangular vertex1= vertex2= vertex3= type="ABSOLUTE"/> in the list's order and orientation; its vertices are <position unit="m"> entries of <define> named <gdml_name>_v<k>, written with 17 digits. axmp-transport --geometry builds a G4TessellatedSolid of G4TriangularFacet (ABSOLUTE, lengths in m) set closed, after checking that the triangles close (every edge once in each direction) and enclose a positive volume: Geant4 only warns about an open solid, the program refuses it as an invalid file (exit status 2, the solid named).

    Every 3D volume is tessellated, including the primitives: a native solid that touches a tessellated one across a curved face would overlap it by the sagitta, which Geant4's overlap check (a tolerance of zero) refuses.

    The solids¶

    Each face becomes one solid, a full revolution about the z axis:

    1. Notches at the axis are filled. The solid of a face is the revolution of its outer loop with every notch at the axis filled: of the parts of the loop between consecutive points on the axis, the one that runs upwards is kept and the others are replaced by the axis. Whatever lies in a notch (a face, or a hole of the domain) becomes a daughter of the solid, as whatever lies in a hole does. This is what makes a sphere on the axis exact: the face around it is a full tube, and the sphere is placed in it.
    2. The kind of solid. One closed circle off the axis is a torus; one half circle centred on the axis, its ends on the axis, is an orb; straight edges that make a rectangle (after merging collinear edges) a tube; anything else a genericPolycone, exact when its edges are straight and discretized when it has arcs. A circle split into several curves, an ellipse and a fillet are polycones.
    3. Holes of the domain are vacuum. The boundary curves of the domain, followed with the domain on their left, form closed chains (or chains from the axis to the axis, closed along it). A chain that turns clockwise encloses a part of the exterior: it becomes a vacuum solid void_<k> (G4_Galactic), so that a hole is never filled with the material around it.
    4. The mother of a face or a void is the smallest filled loop that contains it; one that nothing contains is a daughter of the world. Siblings do not overlap and every daughter lies inside its mother by construction, provided no two curves come closer than about the chord tolerance (the chords of a curve stray from it by up to its sagitta). The conversion checks it: when a layer is thinner than that, so that a face or a void would lose its place in the tree, a daughter would leave its mother, two siblings would overlap or a polycone would cross itself, it refuses the partition with particle.partition_invalid, whose hint is to lower chord_tol_m well below the layer's thickness. It never writes a list without a face or with overlapping solids. Curves that touch convert when the discretized chords stay on their own side (two circles or ellipses touching from outside, a torus on the edge of a box, an ellipse inscribed in a box). Where a discretized curve is concave towards the other solid (a shape touching the inside of an ellipse, say), the chords pass through that solid by about 0.8 of the chord tolerance at every tolerance, so lowering chord_tol_m does not help: the partition is refused, and the curves must be separated there by a few chord tolerances. A face whose outline passes twice through one point (a pinch: a notch whose tip touches the face's own boundary) is refused too, naming the region and the point, because whether Geant4 builds such a polycone depends on rounding in its crossing test; separate the touching boundaries. The checks work to 1e-12 of the partition's diagonal but never above 5e-13 m (half of Geant4's overlap tolerance), so that a partition admitted here is not refused by Geant4's overlap check.
    5. The world is a vacuum tube (G4_Galactic) centred at the origin, enclosing the partition with a margin of 1e-3 of its diagonal.
    6. Names. The solids of a region are <region>_0, <region>_1, ... in a geometric order (their bounding box, their area, then their vertices); the voids void_0, void_1, ... (skipping a name that the solids of a region called void already have); the world world. Any region or material name is accepted: the names that GDML and Geant4 use are mapped from them (gdml_name, below).

    Discretization. Every arc of a polycone is cut into chords once, from its curve, and every solid that has it uses the same points, so neighbours share their vertices bit for bit and leave no gap or sliver. The end points are the partition's; the points between are at equal steps of the curve parameter. A circular arc of radius R and sweep θ is cut into the fewest equal angles N whose sagitta R (1 - cos(θ/2N)) is within the tolerance; an elliptical arc uses the same rule with its major semi-axis, whose sagitta bounds the true one. The tolerance chord_tol of a region is min(1 µm, 1e-5 × the diagonal of the box of its curves); a curve between two regions uses the smaller of their two; chord_tol_m= sets every region's. Tori and orbs are never discretized.

    solids.json¶

    {"schema": "axionmultiphysics.solids/1", "generator": "axionmultiphysics <version>", "geometry": "axisymmetric",
 "units": {"length": "m", "angle": "deg", "density": "kg/m3", "volume": "m3", "mass": "kg"},
 "chord_tol_m": null, "world": "world", "materials": [...], "solids": [...], "regions": {...}, "warnings": []}

    The file holds no time, host name or path, and the same partition gives the same bytes on every run (strict JSON, UTF-8, numbers as the shortest text that reads back as the same double).

    • materials: the materials that some region uses, sorted by name: {"name", "gdml_name", "density_kg_m3", "composition"} and, when given, mean_excitation_eV, state, T_K, pressure_Pa. composition is {"nist": "G4_..."} or {"elements": {symbol: value}, "by": "mass" | "atoms"}.
    • solids: every solid after its mother (the world first, children in name order), each {"name", "gdml_name", "kind", "params", "mother", "position_m", "rotation_deg", "region", "material", "discretized", "sagitta_m"}. region and material are the document's names. position_m is relative to the mother; rotation_deg is [0, 0, 0] for an axisymmetric design. region is null for the world and the voids, whose material is G4_Galactic. sagitta_m is the largest sagitta of the chords in the solid (0 when none).
    • The parameters of each kind are named as GDML's attributes, with a unit suffix:
    kind params frame
    tube rmin_m, rmax_m, z_m (the full length), startphi_deg, deltaphi_deg its centre
    torus rmin_m (0), rmax_m, rtor_m, startphi_deg, deltaphi_deg the centre of its circle
    orb r_m its centre
    genericPolycone startphi_deg, deltaphi_deg, rz_m ([[r, z], ...], counter-clockwise) the origin: its points are absolute
    tessellated (3D lists) vertices_m ([[x, y, z], ...]), triangles ([[i, j, k], ...], counter-clockwise seen from outside) the origin: its vertices are absolute

    Values that the partition states (radii, vertices) are copied bit for bit. - regions: per region with faces, in document order: material, density_kg_m3, solids (its solids' names), volume_m3, volume_solids_m3, volume_rel_diff, mass_kg, mass_solids_kg, mass_rel_diff, sagitta_max_m, chord_tol_m. - warnings: one entry {"code": "particle.volume_deficit", "region", ...} per region above the threshold.

    The volume and mass report¶

    volume_m3 is the volume of the exact solid of revolution of the region's faces: by Pappus' theorem, π times the closed integral of r² dz around every loop of every face, evaluated in closed form on lines and arcs from the curve parameters. volume_solids_m3 is what the solids hold: for each of the region's solids, its volume minus its daughters' (the closed forms Geant4 uses: a tube, a torus, a sphere, and the sum of cone frusta of a polycone). volume_rel_diff is volume_solids_m3 / volume_m3 - 1; it is zero to rounding for exact solids, and for discretized ones the chords move the volume by at most 2π r_max s L (s the sagitta, L the length of the curved boundary). The masses are the volumes times the material's density. When |volume_rel_diff| or |mass_rel_diff| exceeds VOLUME_WARN (1e-4), the region is warned about (SolidsWarning, message particle.volume_deficit: region '<name>' ...) and listed in warnings: lower chord_tol_m.

    Materials¶

    A material in the transport's domain needs a particle block and a density; nothing is ever treated as vacuum (vacuum is written explicitly, as {"nist": "G4_Galactic"}):

    "iron":  {"density": 7874.0, "particle": {"nist": "G4_Fe"}},
"steel": {"density": 7930.0, "particle": {"elements": {"Fe": 0.70, "Cr": 0.19, "Ni": 0.11}, "by": "mass"}},
"pe":    {"density": 940.0,  "particle": {"elements": {"C": 1, "H": 2}, "by": "atoms", "mean_excitation_eV": 57.4}}
    • nist: the name of a NIST material of Geant4 (G4_...); the material is that composition at the document's density.
    • elements with by: "mass": mass fractions, positive and summing to 1 within 1e-9 (they are not renormalized); with by: "atoms": positive integer atom counts. Elements are given by their symbols, from H to Bh (Z = 1 to 107, the elements Geant4's NIST tables build); a heavier element is refused, so that no exported file names an element Geant4 cannot build.
    • Optional: mean_excitation_eV, state (solid, liquid or gas), T_K, pressure_Pa (positive).
    • NIST compositions are resolved before a run. from_partition writes a NIST composition as a material of one component (the NIST material) at the document's density, and Geant4 would then compute its mean excitation energy from the elements by Bragg additivity and set its state from the density, instead of using NIST's values (water 69.0 eV, solid, instead of 78.0 eV, liquid; NaI 400 eV instead of 452 eV). The transport therefore resolves every NIST composition against the Geant4 it runs with (particles.resolve_materials, which reads axmp-transport --nist; the driver runs it before every run and writes the resolved list to the run's exchange/solids.json):
      • a material whose block is exactly {"nist": X} at X's own density (within 1e-9, relative) becomes the NIST material itself: its solids name X, the GDML file references X, and the material record is dropped when no solid uses it (the region keeps its document material name in regions);
      • any other NIST composition keeps its one component and takes X's mean_excitation_eV and state where the document gives none (written as <MEE> and state= in GDML, set the same way natively);
      • a density more than 1 % from NIST's warns particle.density_differs_from_nist (the document's density is used).

    The residual of the second case, measured with Geant4 11.4.3's G4EmCalculator (total dE/dx at NIST's density against the NIST material itself):

    material 1 GeV/c muons 100 GeV/c muons 10 MeV protons 1 MeV electrons
    G4_WATER +1.01 % 0.00 % +0.58 % +0.61 %
    G4_SODIUM_IODIDE +1.41 % +0.06 % +1.23 % +0.45 %
    G4_CESIUM_IODIDE +1.69 % +0.04 % +1.61 % +0.40 %
    G4_BGO +1.99 % +0.10 % 0 +1.18 %
    G4_POLYSTYRENE +0.82 % 0 +1.05 % +0.79 %
    G4_KAPTON +1.02 % 0 +0.81 % +0.98 %
    G4_AIR, G4_PbWO4, G4_lAr within 0.09 % within 0.09 % within 0.09 % within 0.09 %
    G4_Fe, G4_Si 0 0 +0.16 %, +0.21 % 0

    It comes from data Geant4 looks up by the NIST material's name (the density-effect table, ICRU 90 water, proton stopping tables), which only the NIST material itself carries. Where it matters, use the NIST density, or give an explicit element composition with mean_excitation_eV. - A document material may not have a name that starts with G4_: that prefix always names a NIST material. - The density is the material's density in kg/m3 and is always the one used. (Comparing it with the NIST density of a NIST material is a check of the transport set-up, where Geant4's tables are.)

    GDML¶

    write_gdml(doc, path) writes the same list as GDML (define, materials, solids, structure, setup), so that any Geant4 application can read it. Three rules make Geant4 read from it the same numbers as axmp-transport builds from solids.json:

    1. 17 significant digits. Every number is written with 17 significant digits (%.17g), which reads back as the same double (Geant4's own writer uses 15, which does not). Lengths are in m and angles in deg, units Geant4 multiplies exactly as the native path does; a density is <D value=... unit="kg/m3"/> with the document's value.
    2. NIST by name, never defined. The file defines no element, no isotope and no material with a NIST name: Geant4's reader prefers a file's definition to its NIST table, which would change the material's data. Every document material is a material of the file, named by its gdml_name, with its density and its components: one <fraction n="1" ref="G4_Fe"/> for a NIST composition, <fraction n=... ref="Fe"/> by mass, <composite n=... ref="H"/> by atoms (Geant4's NIST manager resolves the elements from their symbols), after T, P, MEE and D. The world and the voids reference G4_Galactic; so does every solid whose material a resolved list names directly (G4_WATER at NIST's density, say): its volume's materialref is the NIST name.
    3. Rotations are written as the document's angles, x then y then z (none in an axisymmetric design).

    One volume per solid, named by the solid's gdml_name, is defined after its daughters (the world last); each daughter is a physvol with a position in m. The volume's solid, placement and position are named after it, with _solid, _pv and _pos.

    Names in the file. A GDML name is an XML ID: ASCII letters, digits, _, . and -, not starting with a digit, unique across the file; and Geant4's reader cuts a name where it contains 0x. A document may name its regions and materials as it likes (316L steel, 1st, world), so each solid and each material carries a gdml_name: its own name when that is valid, contains no 0x, does not start with G4_ and collides with no other name of the file (including the _solid, _pv and _pos names, and Default, the name of the file's setup); otherwise the name with every other character replaced by _, a _ in front of a leading digit, 0x written 0_x, and _1, _2, ... appended until it is unique. Solids are named before materials, each set in name order, so the mapping depends only on the names, never on the order of the input. axmp-transport gives its Geant4 volumes and materials the same names, and its placements the names Geant4's GDML reader gives them (<gdml_name>_pv, and <gdml_name>_PV for the world's), so a GDML application and the native path name everything alike; solids.json keeps the document's names beside them. With these names the file follows the GDML schema of Geant4 11.4.3 (the schema is not part of this repository).

    The geometry mode of axmp-transport¶

    axmp-transport --geometry solids.json

    builds the list in Geant4 (see Particle transport for building the program): the materials through Geant4's NIST manager as above (a NIST composition is a material of the document's density with one component, the NIST material; a solid whose material is a NIST name uses that NIST material), one G4Tubs, G4Torus, G4Orb, G4GenericPolycone or (3D lists) G4TessellatedSolid per solid, one logical volume per solid placed in its mother's, with the lengths read in metres and the angles in degrees, and every volume and material named by its gdml_name. It then runs Geant4's overlap check (CheckOverlaps, 1000 points per placement, a tolerance of zero) on every placement and prints one JSON document on stdout:

    {"ok": true,
 "volumes": [{"name", "g4_name", "placement", "region", "material", "solid_volume_m3", "volume_m3", "density_kg_m3", "mass_kg"}],
 "materials": [{"name", "g4_name", "density_kg_m3", "mean_excitation_eV", "state", "T_K", "pressure_Pa",
                "elements"}],
 "overlaps": []}

    name, region and material are the names of the solids list, g4_name the name the Geant4 volume or material holds (the gdml_name; a NIST material keeps its name) and placement the name of the volume's placement. solid_volume_m3 is Geant4's volume of the solid, volume_m3 that minus its daughters', density_kg_m3 the density of its Geant4 material and mass_kg their product. They agree with the report's volume_solids_m3 and mass_solids_kg within 1e-9. materials lists every material the volumes use (G4_Galactic included) as Geant4 built it: its density, mean excitation energy (the document's mean_excitation_eV, or the one Geant4 computes from the elements), state (solid, liquid or gas; Geant4 makes a material of undefined state solid above 10 kg/m3 and a gas below), temperature and pressure (293.15 K and 101325 Pa unless the document gives them) and elements, the mass fraction of each element. These are what a GDML application reading the exported file must reproduce. Every value is converted from Geant4's units once, on output.

    An overlap refuses the geometry: exit status 3 and {"ok": false, "code": "particle.geometry_overlap", "error": ..., "overlaps": [{"volume", "mother", "message"}]}. A file that is not a valid solids list is refused with exit status 2, a message on stderr and nothing on stdout; that includes a polycone that Geant4 refuses to build (an outline of almost no area, edges that cross, or fewer than three points left after Geant4 merges the vertices closer than its length tolerance, 1e-12 m, in both R and Z; Geant4's own message is on stderr) or one whose outline runs back over itself along a stretch of an edge (the program's own check). --check reports "geometry": true for a program that has this mode.

    Codes¶

    code kind raised for
    particle.planar_not_offered error A planar partition: transport is offered for axisymmetric and 3D designs.
    particle.partition_unavailable error Design.export_gdml on a design whose partition cannot be exported in this version or with this mesher: a 3D design reduced by symmetry planes (mesh.symmetry), an imported mesh (mesh.file), or an axmp-mesher without --export-regions.
    particle.partition_invalid error A table that does not describe closed faces with exact curves (see The partition), or a layer thinner than about the chord tolerance, whose solids would overlap, cross themselves or lose their place in the tree (lower chord_tol_m).
    particle.composition_missing error A region without a material, a material that is not defined, or a material without a particle block.
    particle.density_missing error A material used by a region without a positive, finite density.
    particle.composition_invalid error A particle block that is not one of the forms above: an unknown key, both or neither of nist and elements, a nist name without G4_, by missing or not mass/atoms, an unknown element symbol or one heavier than Bh (Z = 107), mass fractions that are not positive or do not sum to 1, atom counts that are not positive integers, a state other than solid, liquid or gas, a non-positive mean_excitation_eV, T_K or pressure_Pa, or a document material named G4_...; for Design.export_gdml also a composition density that differs from the design's.
    particle.volume_deficit warning A region whose solids differ from the exact solid of revolution by more than VOLUME_WARN in volume or mass.
    particle.geometry_overlap error axmp-transport --geometry: Geant4's overlap check found a placement that leaves its mother or enters a sibling.
    particle.density_differs_from_nist warning particles.resolve_materials: a NIST composition whose density differs from NIST's by more than 1 %.

    The messages name the region, the material, the element or the key concerned.

    Limits of this version¶

    • Axisymmetric designs (full revolutions only) and 3D designs without symmetry planes; model documents come later.
    • A 3D design is exported as tessellated solids only (native solids for primitives are not offered), to the chord tolerance of the export. A layer thinner than the chord tolerance is refused by the conversion (see "Thin layers" in 3D designs); a very thin layer needs a fine chord_tol_m and a large triangulation.
    • The partition must carry the extended entity table (exact curve parameters and face loops). B-splines are not read: the mesher writes a contour's elliptical arc as an ellipse.
    • Design.export_gdml has no mesh.file route, and it meshes an axisymmetric design once to obtain the mesher's input (a 3D design is not meshed: its mesher input is written without the engine).
    • Elements up to Z = 107 only (Geant4's NIST tables build no heavier one).
